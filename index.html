A useful place to start

American regional recipes, explained

American food isn't one single cuisine. Climate, agriculture, immigration, Indigenous foodways, migration, industry, and local traditions have all shaped what people cook across the country.

01

Start with the ingredients

Corn, beans, rice, wheat, seafood, beef, pork, chiles, tomatoes and dairy appear across the country, but regions use them in very different ways.

02

Look at local traditions

Barbecue, seafood cookery, Italian-American food, Cajun and Creole dishes, Tex-Mex cooking and Southwestern chile traditions all developed through distinct histories and communities.

03

Expect overlap

Culinary borders are rarely exact. A dish may travel across state lines, change in a new community, or combine influences from several places.

Our approach: The six regions on Table & Folk are an editorial framework for exploring recipes, not an official map of American cuisine. Recipes are approachable home-cook interpretations rather than claims of one definitive version of a traditional dish.

Before you start cooking

Regional cooking tips

01

Taste as you go

Regional dishes often rely on balance between salt, acid, heat, smoke and richness. Taste before adding more seasoning.

02

Respect the cooking method

Slow smoking, gentle seafood cooking, frying and long simmering produce very different textures. Give the method enough time to work.

03

Make substitutions carefully

Substitutions are useful for home cooks, but changing a key ingredient can also change the character of a dish. Substitute with the recipe's texture and moisture in mind.

Common questions

American regional food FAQ

What is American regional cuisine?

American regional cuisine describes foods and cooking traditions associated with particular parts of the United States. Geography, local ingredients, immigration, agriculture, migration and community traditions all influence regional food.

What are examples of regional American foods?

Examples include Gulf Coast gumbo, Texas barbecue, shrimp and grits, New England lobster rolls, Maryland crab cakes, Chicago deep-dish pizza, New Mexican green chile dishes, California burritos and Pacific Northwest salmon.

What foods are associated with the American South?

Southern food includes many traditions, including barbecue, shrimp and grits, hot chicken, biscuits, cornbread and dishes influenced by Cajun, Creole, Lowcountry and African American cooking.

What foods are associated with New England?

New England is strongly associated with seafood and coastal cooking, including lobster rolls, clam chowder, oysters, baked fish and other dishes shaped by the Atlantic coastline.

What makes Southwestern food different?

Southwestern food reflects Indigenous, Mexican, Spanish and later American influences. Chiles, corn, beans, tortillas and beef are common, with regional ingredients such as New Mexico green chile playing an important role.

Are your six regions official geographic regions?

No. The six regions used by Table & Folk are an editorial framework for exploring the recipes. Real American culinary traditions overlap, and food boundaries do not always follow state lines.
